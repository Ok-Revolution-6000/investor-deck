Company purpose

Give every independent brand same-day delivery, at the price of next week's.

We are building the fulfilment layer for the long tail of commerce: a network of small, automated warehouses inside cities, shared by thousands of brands.

Problem

Shoppers moved to same-day.
Small brands could not follow.

Most shoppers now expect delivery within two days, and many within one. The average independent brand still ships in four.

I.

Slow by default

One warehouse, hundreds of miles from the customer. Ground shipping takes four days, and the shopper has already bought elsewhere.

II.

Priced out

Same-day carriers want volumes only the giants reach. Small brands pay retail rates for speed, or don't offer it at all.

III.

Locked in

Fulfilment contracts run for years, with set-up fees, storage minimums and penalties to leave. Growth means renegotiating.

IV.

Flying blind

Stock sits in one place while demand appears in twenty. No one tells the brand which city needs which product next week.

Problem · the operator's side

City warehouses sit half empty while brands wait.

Small logistics operators hold the space brands need, but not the orders. Between seasonal contracts, bays sit idle and staff wait for the next truck.

“We have the space. We don't have the orders.”Operator interviews, illustrative

I.

Idle

Urban warehouses run at a fraction of capacity outside peak season, and pay rent on every empty bay.

II.

Unpredictable

One large client leaves and a third of the revenue goes with it. There is no market to refill the space.

III.

Manual

Clipboards, spreadsheets and phone calls. Automation is sold in million-dollar projects built for giants.

IV.

Disconnected

No link to the storefronts where orders begin, so operators can't sell same-day to the brands that want it.
